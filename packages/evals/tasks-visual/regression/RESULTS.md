# Visual quality gate — before/after evidence

Every number here was produced by the live service (`POST /api/admin/critique`) against real
renders, not estimated.

## The two fixtures

| | `golem-plaza-baseline` | `golem-plaza-improved` |
|---|---|---|
| Origin | captured from the live Studio place the owner rejected (placeId 123864611037141) | generated by applying the rules in `apps/worker/src/worldbuilding.ts` |
| Parts | 17 | 219 |
| Materials | Plastic×7, Neon×5, Metal×3, Slate×2 | Rock×73, Cobblestone×47, Metal×45, Marble×18, Wood×15, Grass×8, Neon×6, Brick×4, Fabric×2, WoodPlanks×1 |
| Ground | 2048-stud default Baseplate plus one 40×1×40 slab at Roblox's factory grey (163,162,165) | 47 cobblestone tiles in 3 stone tones, a 60-segment curb ring, entry steps |
| Tallest element | 12 studs (a lamp post) | 23 studs (the monument) |
| **Visual score** | **2 / 10** | **5 / 10** |
| Passes the gate (≥6) | no | no |
| Cost per critique | 63 neurons ($0.0007) | 72 neurons ($0.0008) |

## What the critic said, unprompted

Baseline:

> This is programmer art: a flat grey slab, four bare cylinders, a floating brown rectangle, a
> floating blue cube and a sub-stud-scale trophy. Every requested object is technically present and
> nothing else is.

That is the owner's own complaint, reached independently from pixels. It also caught two things the
owner did not mention: the `GlmMarker` neon cube floating unattached above the trophy, and the sign
sitting edge-on to the approach, so it is unreadable from the spawn.

Improved:

> This is a competent layout, not a good scene. The circular plan, cobblestone tile variation, and
> mixed materials show real intent, and the monument-lamp-bench spacing is readable. But it floats
> on a featureless green plane, the mid-ring of the plaza is empty, [and] the lamps are unlit sticks
> with no light instances at a 16.5 clock time.

Both fixtures fail the gate, which is correct — the improved scene genuinely has no `PointLight`
instances and no surrounding terrain. **A gate that passed the second scene would be miscalibrated.**

## Three defects this loop found in its own tooling

The gate was pointed at itself before it was trusted. Each of these was a real bias that would have
corrupted every score it ever produced:

1. **Framing.** Cameras sat at 2.1× the subject radius, so scenes filled 8–11% of the frame. The
   critic correctly called it "a postage stamp in a void" — a finding about the *renderer*, not the
   scene. Fixed to 1.35×; subject coverage rose to roughly a third of the frame.
2. **Resolution.** At 176×112 the critic could not resolve mid-scale props: it reported "no benches,
   no planters" for a scene containing six benches and four planters, and hallucinated "three grey
   box buildings" that do not exist. Raised to 288×180 and the hallucination stopped.
3. **Lighting bias.** The rasteriser draws one fixed sun with no shadows, `PointLight`s, Neon glow or
   post-processing. The critic was therefore marking *every* scene down for "zero cast shadows" —
   a constant, not a signal. Lighting is now reported as configuration
   (`Lighting.Brightness`/`ClockTime`/`Ambient`, light-instance count, effects present) and the
   critic is told explicitly not to judge it from the images. It now reports the real fault ("no
   light instances at ClockTime 16.5") instead of the renderer artefact.

## Honest limitations

- The renderer draws boxes with per-face flat shading. `MeshPart`s, `SpecialMesh`es, `Terrain`,
  decals, textures and particles are **not** drawn. A scene built largely from meshes will be
  under-represented in its own critique.
- No shadows and no light transport, hence the configuration-based lighting judgement above.
- A score is one model's opinion. The hard-fail checks are the part that cannot be flattered: they
  are computed from geometry and lighting properties, and any one of them caps the score at 3.
- Two fixtures is not a calibration set. The scores are reproducible, not yet validated against
  human ranking at scale.

# Ladder run, 2026-08-31 — real agent builds against live Studio

Driven by `packages/evals/src/visual-bench.mjs` through the production admin endpoints: same agent,
same tools, same prompts, same quota, same budget. Each round hands the agent its own critique.

| task | trajectory | round 1 | round 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| `b1-lamp-post` | **5 → 6** | 69 parts, 4 materials, 1 light, 336s | **119 parts, 2 lights**, 220s |
| `b2-market-stall` | 4 → 4 | 121 parts, 7 materials, 1 light, 267s | unchanged, 110s |
| `b3-plaza` | not scored | **725 parts, 10 materials, 5 lights**, 355s | — daily cap reached |
| `b4-interior` | not run | — | — |

Round 2 of the lamp is the clearest evidence the correction loop closes: handed its own critique,
the agent nearly doubled the part count and added a second light, and the independent re-score moved
5 → 6. The critic's complaints moved with it — from "there is a stray concrete slab and no mullions"
to "the column does not taper and the base lacks ornament", a strictly deeper class of defect.

## The plaza is the important result, and it is a negative one

`b3-plaza` produced **725 parts across 10 materials with 5 lights and 6 lighting effects**, in a
123 × 48.5 × 123 stud footprint — against the rejected baseline's 17 parts, 4 materials and 0 lights.
A 40× increase in geometry.

It still does not look good. See `bench-plaza/contact-sheet.png` (hero / top / eye):

- The **top-down view is 100% uniform grey** (`subjectCoverage: 1.0`). The ground is one flat
  untextured plate spanning the entire frame with no tonal variation, no paving pattern and no
  material break.
- The 725 parts went into small props around the perimeter and a central spire. Massing, vertical
  variation and ground treatment — the things that actually decide whether a space reads — did not
  change.
- From eye level the scene is thin: a ring of lamp posts and a monument, with nothing at mid-height
  between the ground and the skyline.

**The lesson: part count is not composition.** The prompt changes successfully bought quantity and
material variety; they did not buy spatial design. The agent has learned to add more things, not yet
to shape a space. That is the next real problem, and it is a different problem from the one that was
just solved.

The plaza was never scored — the global daily neuron cap was reached first, and the cap was left
alone rather than raised. The render above cost nothing: rasterisation happens in the plugin, so
looking at a scene is always free even when judging it is not.

# Bench run, 2026-08-31 (overnight) — two failures worth keeping

Run against the live service and live Studio, after the composition gate, the transcript fix and the
session-affinity change were deployed. Both results are failures and both are informative.

## b4-interior: 4 → 4, and the correction loop did not correct

Previously recorded as "blocked by the daily cap". The cap had reset, so it ran.

| round | time | parts | materials | lights | score |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 377s | 351 | 11 | 9 | 4 |
| 2 | 209s | 351 | 11 | 9 | 4 |

Two things happened, and they are separate faults.

**The agent built the wrong thing.** Asked for a *cosy tavern interior*, it produced a competent
small cottage **exterior**. The critic caught it unprompted: "not the requested cosy tavern interior.
No view shows the bar, stools, tables or fireplace." This is a prompt-fidelity failure, and no
composition metric can or should catch it — `hardFails` was correctly empty, because a cottage with a
roof genuinely does have vertical hierarchy. The visual gate did its job by refusing to pass it.

**Round 2 changed nothing.** Identical part, material and light counts; identical critique, defect
for defect. The agent spent 209 seconds and moved no measurable property. This is the
"patch forever" failure inverted — not endless additive polish, but a correction round that produced
no correction at all. It is the strongest argument in the repo for a rebuild trigger, and it is not
yet fixed.

## b3-plaza: a playtest destroyed the build

| | |
|---|---|
| result | `ERR — nothing renderable found under game.Workspace`, parts 0 |
| cost | ~1,295 neurons for a run that shipped nothing |

The op log is unambiguous about the ordering:

```
00:13–00:19  run_code, render_view  ok=1     <- geometry existed and rendered
00:27:27     run_mode               ok=1     <- playtest entered
00:27:30     run_mode               ok=1     <- playtest left
00:32:06     render_view            ok=0     <- "nothing renderable found under game.Workspace"
```

Workspace afterwards contained `Baseplate` and `Terrain` and nothing else — the b4 cottage was gone
too. **A playtest cycle appears to discard everything the agent built.** This is not diagnosed: the
mechanism (whether ops were applied to the play DataModel, or the edit DataModel was reverted on
stop) has not been established, and it should not be guessed at. What is established is the ordering
above and the empty Workspace after it.

Checkpoints from before the playtest exist (snapshot ops at 00:15:32 and 00:16:12) so the state is
recoverable; nothing was restored, because which state to restore is the owner's decision.

**Why this matters more than the score:** `start_stop_play` is how the agent is supposed to verify
its own work. If running a playtest deletes the work, then the verification step is destructive and
every playtest-then-fix loop is unwinnable. That is a bigger defect than any composition problem in
this file, and it was found by running the benchmark rather than by reading the code.
