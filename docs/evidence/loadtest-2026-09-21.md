## What this establishes

Twenty concurrent users signed in, created a project, called `/api/me` and held a WebSocket, with
**no failures at any phase**. Six concurrent real inferences returned with **zero errors**. Every
tenant-isolation probe refused correctly while the system was under that load — the cross-project
REST read 404s, the cross-project WebSocket is rejected, an unauthenticated call is 401, admin
without a key is 403, and RLS exposed zero rows.

## What it does NOT establish, and this is the part worth reading

**`p95` over n=6 is the maximum of six samples.** It is printed as a percentile because the script
prints percentiles, and at that sample size the 95th percentile and the largest value are the same
number. So `p95=3224ms` means "the slowest of six was 3.2 s", not "one call in twenty exceeds
3.2 s". Do not compare it to a p95 taken over hundreds of requests.

In particular, **do not read this as a 5x improvement** on SCALE-V2's `p95 17.3s under 6 concurrent,
2.0s median`. That row's median (2.0 s) and this run's median (2.7 s) are in the same place; what
differs is the tail, and a six-sample run is exactly the instrument that cannot see a tail. Two
runs that disagree only in their maximum, at n=6, have not disagreed about anything.

Phase 4 exercises **Clay**, a single question. It is not a build. A quality-gated build is ~16
agent steps plus critiques and is the thing a real user waits on; nothing here times one.

Twenty users is twenty users. The row asks for "roughly tens of simultaneous users" and this is at
the bottom of that range; `infra/loadtest.mjs 30 12` is one command away and costs about twice as
much.

## The row the ledger asked for is now measured at the size it asked for

"Roughly tens of simultaneous users." Thirty signed in, created a project, called `/api/me` and
held a WebSocket **at once**, with no failure at any phase, while twelve real inferences ran and
every one of the seven isolation probes still refused correctly.

## And the two runs do not agree, which is the finding

| | 20 × 6 | 30 × 12 | |
|---|---|---|---|
| auth p50 | 975 ms | **486 ms** | half the latency at 50% more users |
| `/api/me` p50 | 1591 ms | **641 ms** | 2.5× faster at 50% more users |
| project create p50 | 1075 ms | 1131 ms | flat |
| ws connect p50 | 1392 ms | 1213 ms | flat |
| inference p50 | 2702 ms | 2504 ms | flat |

**Load went up and the two authenticated numbers went down by half.** Load does not do that. What
does do that is warmth: `/api/me` reaches a per-user Durable Object, and the 2026-08-30 run recorded
the same effect far larger — p50 3567 ms cold against 130 ms warm, with the note "the cold-start
cost is paid once per user". The 20 × 6 run woke thirty objects that had been idle since August;
the 30 × 12 run an hour later found twenty of them already awake.

So neither of these p50s is a property of the system. **They are a property of how recently the run
before them happened**, and any figure quoted from a single run of this script — including the ones
in `docs/SCALE-V2.md` — is one sample of a bimodal distribution with a 3–5× spread between its
modes. The honest statement this pair supports is the one that does not move: at thirty concurrent
users, across two runs, **nothing failed and nothing leaked**. That is a capacity claim. The
milliseconds are not.

The two figures that did NOT move between the runs — project create (PostgREST + RLS, no Durable
Object) and ws connect — are the ones to trust, and they are flat under a 50% load increase.
