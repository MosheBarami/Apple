## What this establishes

Twenty concurrent users signed in, created a project, called `/api/me` and held a WebSocket, with
**no failures at any phase**. Six concurrent real inferences returned with **zero errors**. Every
tenant-isolation probe refused correctly while the system was under that load — the cross-project
REST read 404s, the cross-project WebSocket is rejected, an unauthenticated call is 401, admin
without a key is 403, and RLS exposed zero rows.

## What it does NOT establish, and this is the part worth reading

**`p95` over n=6 is the maximum of six samples.** It is printed as a percentile because the script
prints percentiles, and at that sample size the 95th percentile and the largest value are the same
number. So `p95=3224ms` means "the slowest of six was 3.2 s", not "one call in twenty exceeds
3.2 s". Do not compare it to a p95 taken over hundreds of requests.

In particular, **do not read this as a 5x improvement** on SCALE-V2's `p95 17.3s under 6 concurrent,
2.0s median`. That row's median (2.0 s) and this run's median (2.7 s) are in the same place; what
differs is the tail, and a six-sample run is exactly the instrument that cannot see a tail. Two
runs that disagree only in their maximum, at n=6, have not disagreed about anything.

Phase 4 exercises **Clay**, a single question. It is not a build. A quality-gated build is ~16
agent steps plus critiques and is the thing a real user waits on; nothing here times one.

Twenty users is twenty users. The row asks for "roughly tens of simultaneous users" and this is at
the bottom of that range; `infra/loadtest.mjs 30 12` is one command away and costs about twice as
much.
