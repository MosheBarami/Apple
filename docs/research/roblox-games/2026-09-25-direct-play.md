# Direct Roblox play observations — 25 September 2026

This is a first-hand visual and interaction log from the signed-in Roblox desktop
player, observed through Computer Use. The first three games were launched from
their official web pages; **Command An Army was selected and launched entirely
inside the Roblox app**. The owner has since required app-only launches. This is
**not** a source-code inspection,
asset licence, gameplay-completion claim, or permission to copy either game's
artwork. Screens were observed in the task transcript; they are not yet archived
as standalone image evidence. The observations below are limited to what appeared
in the player. The linked Roblox pages supply only the labelled product claims.

## Steal An Egg — simulation/tycoon

Official page: https://www.roblox.com/games/107778070777162/Steal-An-Egg

### Observed in the desktop player

- The first camera view placed the avatar inside a fenced green plot. A large
  `Steal an Egg!` heading and repeated, oversized red ground arrows gave an
  immediate direction. The available path was visually separated by brown
  cross-braced fences and dark square posts.
- The plot had a bright green studded floor, a high checkered brown perimeter,
  cyan sky, and large low-poly pet/creature silhouettes. This is a coordinated
  style, rather than isolated props. Large pets belonging to nearby players
  sometimes occluded much of the view; this is an observed usability cost.
- In-world signage showed an `Unlock` offer priced at `$1K`. The persistent HUD
  showed `Shop` and `Index` on the left, egg and pet icons on the right, a
  speed-like `10` and `$0` at bottom left, two numbered quick slots at bottom
  centre, and a timed icon at bottom right. The shop had a red attention badge.
- An event message announced `A FORBIDDEN EXPERIMENT HAS APPEARED!` across the
  lower centre. A Roblox welcome badge appeared after joining. The sky changed
  from day to night and back, and the lower-right timer continued changing.
- The player list showed other users in the same server. Other players and
  oversized pets were visible, so the opening scene did not feel empty.
- I attempted keyboard movement and clicked the visible Shop and egg buttons.
  The movement result was inconclusive and no menu transition was confirmed.
  I did **not** verify stealing, hatching, income, upgrades, purchases, or
  persistence. Leaving the server was confirmed through Roblox's Escape →
  Leave → confirmation flow.

### Claims from the official description, not observed in play

The page describes stealing and hatching eggs, pet income, treadmill/base
upgrades, speed training, stealing from other players, and rarities/mutations.
These are research targets for a later active-play pass; they are not recorded

## RIVALS — round-based shooter

This observation is historical context only. Its restrained arena presentation is
outside Apple's new colorful-cartoon-only product scope; it is not a visual target
for Apple or a source for the new cartoon benchmark.

Official page: https://www.roblox.com/games/17625359962/RIVALS

### Observed in the desktop player

- The loading sequence used a full-screen dark purple/black branded field,
  central logo animation, numeric countdown, a character illustration, and
  rotating tips such as weapon-contract rewards and a shield tactic. It gave
  the wait a purpose rather than exposing a blank loading state.
- A limited-time event screen offered a dense grid of distinct weapon previews.
  The cards combined silhouette, name, border colour and sparkle effects; a
  single short sentence explained the temporary unlock. This was a real
  content selection surface, not a generic button panel.
- The client joined an active round. The in-match HUD put two team scores and
  a timer across the top, kill events at top right, a central reticle, health
  and a spectator count near the bottom, and equipped items at bottom right.
- The avatar was eliminated during this observation. A visible death card
  identified the other player and the weapon. The client entered spectator
  mode with a named teammate, directional controls and the remaining round
  timer. A green `ROUND WON` state appeared when that teammate secured the
  result; the team score changed from 0–0 to 1–0. Later round states reached
  2–2 and 3–2 while the top-right kill feed continued to update. The visible
  bottom HUD changed with the observed player's equipped knife/daggers,
  firearm and grenade, with ammo counts. A red full-screen hit/death tint was
  visible during an elimination.
- The observed arena used pale modular surfaces marked with a regular grid
  and crosshair-like joints. It was visually sparse compared with Steal An
  Egg's colourful plot, yet the match still carried strong readable state,
  weapons, feedback and competitive timing. Do not infer that every RIVALS
  arena has this style from one round.
- I saw weapon silhouettes and a first-person weapon model. I did not verify
  aiming, shooting, damage values, match victory conditions, matchmaking,
  contracts, persistence, or economy by direct action.

### Claims from the official description, not observed in play

The page advertises 1v1–5v5 duels, first to five wins, duel pads, keys,
weapon/skin unlocks, contracts, streaks and leaderboards. These remain
unverified here except that one live round advanced the displayed score.

## Blox Fruits — action RPG opening

Official page: https://www.roblox.com/games/2753915549/Blox-Fruits

### Observed in the desktop player

- The client opened with a blurred, high-colour branded join card, then a
  blue illustrated loading screen that drew a whole archipelago as line art.
  The loading artwork foreshadowed the game's scale and sea travel before the
  world appeared.
- The opening camera looked over a coastal town: multiple roof shapes and
  colours, a tall central domed landmark, streets, blocks of buildings, a
  fountain, a surrounding ocean and distant horizon. The view established an
  authored destination and a sense of geography before asking for input.
- A full-screen `Pick A Side!` choice compared Pirates (red) with Marines
  (blue). Each side used a character portrait, a large faction name, a
  progression measure (`Bounty` or `Honor`) and three brief benefits. `Fast
  Mode` appeared separately at bottom left. Hovering over Pirates enlarged
  and animated that card. The Roblox player list separated the two factions.
- The selection was not confirmed via Computer Use, so subsequent quest,
  combat, travel, level, inventory and fruit interactions were **not**
  observed. The side choice's permanence and any account effects are unknown.

### Claims from the official description, not observed in play

The page describes swords or fruit powers, training, bosses, ocean travel,

## Command An Army — toy-soldier strategy opening (launched in app)

Roblox Home's recommended row was navigated with keyboard arrows. Return opened
the game's detail page; selecting its Play control with keyboard navigation and
pressing Space launched it. No website game page was used for this launch.

- The detail page showed a 95% rating and about 36.5k active players at observation
  time. The thumbnail and loading card used a toy-soldier visual language with
  red/blue shields, yellow Roblox faces, bright green studded ground and cyan sky.
- The opening displayed a blurred branded loading card, then a Tutorial. Space
  advanced a “Press anywhere to continue” prompt into an animated battlefield
  cutscene. The scene had a saturated blue sky, green grass, a castle wall with
  varied crenellation and stylized red/blue soldiers carrying shields.
- After the cutscene, a choice labelled “Are you: [Male] [Female] for voice lines”
  appeared over a blurred green-and-orange field. The two large dark cards were
  the only obvious actions. Computer Use moved/pressed but did not activate them;
  a Roblox keyboard-navigation attempt also produced no confirmed transition.
  A subsequent Computer Use coordinate action returned `noWindowsAvailable` even
  after rebinding to the running Roblox app. The client stayed at this choice.
- No unit command, combat, economy, upgrades, army progression or multiplayer
  gameplay was verified. The cutscene and choice are opening-state evidence only.

This is a relevant colorful-cartoon reference for silhouette, palette and onboarding.
It does not certify that Apple's equivalent mechanics or interface are complete.

## Product and benchmark implications — hypotheses, not pass claims

1. A playable opening needs a safe spawn, a readable objective and a route to
   first action in the same view. A bare spawn plus disconnected geometry fails.
2. Visual style is a *system*: floor/wall/prop silhouettes, colour, typography,
   icon language, light, effects and camera composition must agree. More parts
   alone do not make a game look finished.
3. The opening HUD should be audited for concrete game state and working
   actions. A static decorative ScreenGui is insufficient.
4. The observed games communicate changing state at multiple timescales: immediate
   action feedback, short timers, round/event states and longer progression.
5. A commercial-game benchmark needs first ten seconds, first interaction,
   failed interaction, end-of-loop state and multiplayer/returning-player
   states. One screenshot or one successful Luau test cannot prove quality.
6. Within the colorful-cartoon specialty, simulation, adventure and party modes
   need distinct criteria. Steal An Egg demonstrates world dressing and
   signposting; Blox Fruits uses geographic composition and a prominent faction
   choice; Command An Army uses a toy-soldier palette and cinematic onboarding.
7. Readability under occlusion and in different camera angles needs testing;
   the giant pet in Steal An Egg hid a large part of the playfield.
8. Turn each implication into a scored test only after collecting additional
   games and a reproducible image/video evidence path. Do not train on these
   games' copyrighted images, maps, weapons or UI bytes. Author new task and
   critique examples from the observations and use separately licensed assets.
